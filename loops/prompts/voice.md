Example 1:
As a designer for nonprofits, these are three things you should delete from a website today.
One, PDFs from 2019. Nobody's opening them.
Two, half your menu. Twelve items in your menu could be six.
Three, stock images. Your real people beat strangers in a conference room.
Your site should be a front door, not a filing cabinet.

Example 2:
Do this test on your nonprofit's website right now.
Open your homepage. Pretend you've never heard of your organization. You get five seconds.
Can you answer these three questions? One, what do you do? Two, who is it for? Three, what do I do next?
If not, here's the fix. Put one clear sentence at the top. What do you do and for who?
Add one real picture of your people and one button. Donate, join, volunteer. It's up to you.

Example 3:
If your menu has more than five items, listen to this.
Grab a stack of sticky notes. Write every page of your website on one. Every single one.
Now stick them on a wall and ask yourself two questions: Who is this for? What do they need to do?
Most sites end up with five groups: About, programs and services, how to get involved, events, and donate.
That's your new menu. Anything that doesn't fit, archive and delete it.
Fewer doors mean more people walk through.

Example 4:
Think about the pages you actually follow.
They let you in. You see the work, the people, the messy middle.
And without noticing, you start to feel a part of it too.
Mission-driven teams can build that too. Share the little wins, the people, a random Tuesday morning.
Then, when you need help, you're asking friends. And friends show up.
